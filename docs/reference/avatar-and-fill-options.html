The finding that matters: the hue compensation buys almost nothing at the alphas actually in use. Swapping the base from navfill to the brand value moves the rendered hue by 1.3° when selected and 1.6° on hover — invisible. The compensation was solved for /20, where the drift is real, and nothing on this panel runs at /20. So using the brand value costs no hue accuracy.

But translucency is what desaturates both. Selected renders at 20.3% saturation against the brand's 43.7% — that is why the fill reads washed rather than green, and no change of base fixes it, because the panel underneath is near-white. The opaque hue-locked column is the only one that is actually on the brand hue and the brand saturation.

4 · The button cursor-over value — you can go up, not down

You said 66 was a number you picked so you could move equally either way. You can move up. You cannot move down — 66% is the floor, and the reason is the label, not the fill.

Hover flips the label to the bar's colour. That label has to survive on the fill underneath it. Over the subheader bar, green-800/66 renders #63776b and carries cream at 4.66:1. One step down, /62, is 4.14:1 — under AA at this button's size. So 66 is the lightest value that still works in this direction. Live buttons, hover them:

If you want the hover lighter than 66, the label has to stay green instead of flipping to cream. That is a different move, and it works — but only well below the dead zone:

Between roughly 35% and 60% nothing is legible. At /45 the green label is 5.15:1 and the cream label 2.61:1 — the fill is too dark for green ink and too light for cream. That band is unusable whichever label you choose, which is why there is no middle setting to tune toward.
